# Open questions — deliberately not resolved

1. What is the weakest task-relative equivalence criterion permitting safe reuse after representational drift?
2. When is a generative seed more useful than storing a static abstraction or exact construction history?
3. Can a learned trajectory be reparameterized and reused in a genuinely novel context without costly revalidation?
4. Under what conditions does a representation's *partitioning* matter more than its capacity or training data?
5. Can a callable latent transformation outperform a competent program refactorer under equal costs?
6. Does allowing a partial objective to evolve through discovery improve later held-out performance—or just facilitate goal drift?
7. How should a model choose between cached reuse, partial reconstruction, and full recomputation?
8. Are there task families in which nonlinear, context-sensitive composition cannot be usefully approximated with fixed libraries?
9. Can useful knowledge be preserved across model generations without prescribing an identical latent geometry?
10. What future task distribution would reward retaining apparently inefficient or incomplete abstractions?

These questions are retained as possibilities, not claimed results. The broader preservation methodology is discussed separately in [CSDE](https://github.com/leenathomas01/capability-substrate-exploration).
