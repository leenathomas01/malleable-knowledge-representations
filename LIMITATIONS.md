# Limitations, assumptions, and failure modes

- **Non-novel constituents:** program libraries, caching, options, geometric latent transformations, and reversible adapters are established research/engineering families. A new API over them is not automatically a new capability.
- **Identity:** proximity in latent space does not imply functional or causal equivalence; equivalence is probe- and context-relative.
- **Entanglement:** a representation that seems local may depend on much of the surrounding network.
- **Drift and interference:** mild updates can invalidate previously reusable structures; small errors can propagate through dependent uses.
- **Compression:** sufficient information for present behavior may omit distinctions required by a later task.
- **Verification:** cheap proxies may not correlate with held-out benefit; expensive evaluation may erase apparent efficiency gains.
- **Baseline strength:** comparisons must include conventional library refactoring, invalidation and matched extra search.
- **Budget:** retrieval, candidate generation, memory, metric learning and verification all cost resources.
- **Open-endedness:** an unspecified goal does not itself supply an evaluation policy; no proof of open-ended discovery follows.
- **Substrate:** vectors, symbolic programs, manifolds, and dynamical processes are candidate implementations, not established equivalents.
- **Generalization:** a synthetic gain does not establish applicability to frontier models or research programs.
- **Safety:** modularity and rollback reduce some risks, but candidate adoption can still introduce errors; governance remains external.
- **Scientific scope:** results from one implementation can disconfirm its specific prediction, not all imaginable representations.

Maintain explicit uncertainties and publish negative results.
