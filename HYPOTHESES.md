# Hypotheses

## H1 — Valid representational restructuring

Within a specified domain, an optional transformation can produce an alternative arrangement of available representations that passes held-out functional or interventional tests.

**Test:** Compare original and transformed states under preregistered context families and ablation probes. Distinguish behavioral equivalence from complete structural or causal equivalence.

**Weakening result:** Candidates repeatedly fail held-out checks, or only reproduce transformations already provided by a standard refactorer.

## H2 — Net benefit of callable restructuring

With the base model held constant and total compute, retrieval, memory, verification, and search budgets matched, a model equipped with an optional `reshape()` call outperforms competent non-reshape baselines on predetermined post-shift compositional tasks.

**Primary outcome:** Held-out task success under a fixed total budget. Report compute/cost separately; do not use an arbitrary composite score without preregistration.

**Strong baselines:** Ordinary additional search; retrieval and reasoning; compiler-style library refactoring and invalidation; optionally a fixed learned latent operator and same-extra-compute adaptation.

**Weakening result:** Gains vanish against the best baseline after cost equalization, or disappear on held-out contexts.

## H3 — Exploration without a fixed initial target (future work)

An optional, constrained exploration mode discovers reusable transformations that improve performance on future *unannounced* tasks compared with fixed-task search and diversity-preserving search under matched resources.

This is separate from H1 and H2 and is **not tested by Experiment 001**.

## Universal caution

A finite null result is domain-specific. A positive small-scale test is not evidence of general intelligence, open-ended scientific discovery, or persistent model self-reorganization. An explanation or geometric similarity is not proof of causal fidelity.
